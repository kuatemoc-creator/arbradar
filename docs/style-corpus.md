# Style corpus: how the arbitration trade press writes

Collected 24 September 2026 from the reader's inbox (100 GAR daily briefings,
May–September 2026; 50 IAReporter "Latest Headlines" mailings, May–July 2024),
nine mailings read in full, and the GAR feed archive of 100 stories with their
summaries (`docs/style-corpus.json`). This is the reference for ArbRadar's
headlines, explanations and structure.

## How GAR's daily briefing is built

- One email a day, sent between 19:00 and 22:00 UTC, subject = the lead headline.
- Two sections only: **TODAY'S HEADLINES** (four to six items) and **RECENT
  HIGHLIGHTS** (two or three items from the previous days). Nothing else.
- Each item: a headline, then one sentence of 25–45 words (median 32). No
  labels, no tags, no dates per item, no eyebrows. One typeface, one size for
  headlines, one for text. Headlines are links.
- People moves and institutional appointments sit among the headlines, not in
  a separate list ("Reid leaves Debevoise for 3VB", "Willkie names two new
  practice heads", "AAA president joins Clio").
- "BREAKING:" is the only prefix ever used, for a same-day extra mailing.

## How IAReporter's mailing is built

- **Latest Headlines**: five to seven headlines, no summaries. Headlines are
  long and exact (median 15 words), present tense, and name the instrument and
  the forum: "Mining investor lodges ICSID claim against Armenia";
  "Ukraine is put on notice of dispute by state-owned Russian bank";
  "Montenegro seeks counsel for LCIA arbitration over failed tourism project;
  deadline is next week".
- **Previous Headlines**, then **Notable legal topics discussed** (links into a
  topic index), then **Documents for download**: the primary documents behind
  the stories, named "Party v. Party, Document type, Court, Date".
- Prefixes: "Analysis:", "Looking Back:", "[Updated]", "Round-Up".

## The headline grammar (GAR, 100 leads, median 7 words)

Party + verb + object, sentence case, present tense, no colon, no quotation
marks except for a quoted word, an amount only when it is the point.

- Wins and losses: "Ghana liable in desalination plant dispute"; "Slovenia
  defeats claim over fracking ban"; "Nigeria beats Burford-backed hydropower
  claim"; "Lebanese businessman loses claim against Netherlands"; "Armenia
  declares victory over waste management claim"; "France and Spain win rail saga".
- Filings and threats: "CK Hutchison launches treaty claim against Panama";
  "Lithium miner launches ICSID claim against Nigeria"; "Chinese steelmaker
  threatens claim against UK"; "Businessman threatens Armenia over “political”
  seizure of assets"; "Casino investors file second claim against Ecuador";
  "Czech Republic faces new claim over blood plasma saga".
- Courts: "UK judge sides with Russia on estoppel in Yukos Capital case";
  "Russia loses Singapore immunity appeal in Yukos saga"; "US court sends
  medical glove dispute to arbitration"; "Mexico fails to overturn NAFTA award in
  Canada"; "Libyan court declares billion-dollar award void"; "Indian court
  denies asset freeze based on Russian judgment debt".
- Enforcement: "Libyan state entity fails to lift French attachment";
  "Telefónica seeks to enforce award against Colombia"; "Turkish contractor
  enforces against Georgia"; "Peru pays electricity tender award"; "Malaysian
  airline's assets seized in Singapore".
- Annulment and challenges: "Italy seeks to annul Veolia award"; "Chinese food
  group's award annulled in Chile"; "German arbitrator survives challenge in case
  against Austria"; "Iran's oil company challenges top judges in Crescent case".
- Institutions and policy: "UNCITRAL eases enforcement of awards in electronic
  form"; "SIAC opens in Delhi"; "European Commission probes another renewables
  award"; "US Supreme Court won't hear Spain and Russia's immunity appeals".
- People: "Freshfields' Saudi disputes head joins Three Crowns in Dubai";
  "Born takes WilmerHale team to King & Spalding"; "US State Department lawyer
  rejoins Pillsbury"; "Ukraine instructs boutique to defend US investor's ICSID
  claim".
- Verbs that carry the corpus: wins, loses, defeats, beats, seeks, threatens,
  launches, files, lodges, brings, pursues, enforces, pays, settles, fails to,
  faces, avoids, upholds, annuls, sides with, sends, orders, joins, leaves,
  names, instructs, takes, rejoins, opens, probes.

## The summary grammar (GAR, 99 summaries, median 32 words)

One sentence. Subject is the party described by nationality and kind, or the
State, or the court. Then the act, the forum or instrument, the amount, and one
clause of context.

- "A Spanish construction company says it has won US$235 million in an ICC
  dispute with Ghana and a state utility over a desalination plant in Accra."
- "Italy has applied to annul an Energy Charter Treaty award that required it
  to pay €86 million to French environmental services group Veolia for
  breaching two waste treatment concessions."
- "A Malaysian infrastructure company has reportedly threatened to bring a
  US$125 million investment treaty claim against India, alleging that judicial
  delays have prevented it from collecting on an arbitral award issued 12 years
  ago."
- "Ukraine has instructed European boutique Queritius to defend it in an ICSID
  claim brought by a US distressed debt investor that the state alleges is
  linked to Russia."
- "Amani Khalifa has left her role as head of the Saudi Arabia disputes practice
  at Freshfields to join the partnership at Three Crowns in Dubai."
- "A Kuwaiti company seeking to enforce a billion-dollar award against Libya has
  persuaded a Paris court to uphold an attachment of assets belonging to the
  state's national oil company."
- "Hong Kong-based conglomerate CK Hutchison Holdings has made good on its threat
  to file a US$1.5 billion investment treaty claim against Panama over a
  cancelled port concession – adding to a US$2 billion ICC case its subsidiary is
  pursuing over the same dispute."
- "An arm of Libya's central bank has launched an ICSID claim against Burkina
  Faso over the nationalisation of their local banking joint venture."

Habits worth copying: "says it has won" and "reportedly" mark unverified
claims; "made good on its threat" links a filing to an earlier threat; the
amount comes with its currency and the forum with its acronym; the closing
clause explains what the dispute is about, never why the reader should care.

## What the two do not do

No adjectives of importance, no "significant" or "landmark". No explanation
of why it matters. No calls to action. No lists of tags. No source lines: the
publication is the source, and its links go to its own articles.
